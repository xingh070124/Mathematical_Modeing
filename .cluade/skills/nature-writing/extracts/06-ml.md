## Paper 6: Romera-Paredes, B., Barekatain, M., Novikov, A., Balog, M., Kumar, M. P., Dupont, E., Ruiz, F. J. R., Ellenberg, J. S., Wang, P., Fawzi, O., Kohli, P. & Fawzi, A. "Mathematical discoveries from program search with large language models." Nature 625, 468–475 (2024).
- url_oa: https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/funsearch-making-new-discoveries-in-mathematical-sciences-using-large-language-models/Mathematical-discoveries-from-program-search-with-large-language-models.pdf (author preprint of accepted Nature version)
- journal: Nature
- year: 2024
- category: method (LLM + evolutionary search for scientific discovery)

A1_title: "Mathematical discoveries from program search with large language models"
A2_abstract_map:
  - S1 (capability frame) — "Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated tremendous capabilities in solving complex tasks, from quantitative reasoning to understanding natural language."
  - S2 (problem) — "However, LLMs sometimes suffer from confabulations (or hallucinations) which can result in them making plausible but incorrect statements."
  - S3 (gap consequence) — "This hinders the use of current large models in scientific discovery."
  - S4 (here-we) — "Here we introduce FunSearch (short for searching in the function space), an evolutionary procedure based on pairing a pre-trained LLM with a systematic evaluator."
  - S5 (claim) — "We demonstrate the effectiveness of this approach to surpass the best known results in important problems, pushing the boundary of existing LLM-based approaches."
  - S6 (concrete result 1) — "Applying FunSearch to a central problem in extremal combinatorics — the cap set problem — we discover new constructions of large cap sets going beyond the best known ones, both in finite dimensional and asymptotic cases."
  - S7 (significance) — "This represents the first discoveries made for established open problems using LLMs."
  - S8 (concrete result 2) — "We showcase the generality of FunSearch by applying it to an algorithmic problem, online bin packing, finding new heuristics that improve upon widely used baselines."
  - S9 (methodological insight) — "In contrast to most computer search approaches, FunSearch searches for programs that describe how to solve a problem, rather than what the solution is."

## Cross-paper observations

- **Pivot phrasing is heterogeneous, not always "Here we"**: ChemCrow uses "We introduce", MolE uses "we report", FunSearch uses "Here we introduce", AlphaGeometry uses "We propose", SemanticLens uses "This paper introduces", Pai et al. uses no abstract-level pivot at all (definitional opener). The "Here we" canonical Nature stem is most strongly preserved in DeepMind-authored Nature papers (FunSearch, AlphaGeometry-with-"We propose").

- **Abstracts in this category run long when the paper has multiple discoveries** — FunSearch (10 sentences) vs MolE (6 sentences). Each additional sentence carries one extra named result, never a mere restatement. The pattern: capability → flaw → here-we → mechanism → result-1 → result-2 (optional) → meta-property → impact.

- **"The benchmark is the result"** — all six papers anchor their abstract's strongest claim to a *named external benchmark* (TDC ADMET leaderboard, IMO problem set, cap-set capacity bound, OR-Library bin packing, three clinical use cases, ABCDE-rule audit). Even the interpretability paper (SemanticLens) uses a clinical rule (ABCDE) as the audit anchor.

- **Section-header taxonomy splits two ways**: (i) **task-numbered use-case scheme** (Pai et al.: "use case 1/2/3"; FunSearch: "Extremal combinatorics" / "Bin packing"), and (ii) **claim-anchored noun-phrase headers** (MolE: "MolE embeddings *are* a meaningful representation"; ChemCrow: "Autonomous chemical synthesis"). Method-named headers ("Architecture", "Training") are absent — these papers prefer claim/task labels.

- **Limitations are increasingly *enumerated* rather than buried**. Pai et al. and FunSearch both deliver named-bullet limitations (retrospective/demographics/clinical-data integration; evaluator-availability/rich-scoring/skeleton-isolatability). MolE delivers a single-clause "to be determined" hedge. ChemCrow names reproducibility-under-API as the first limitation. The trend in 2024-25 NMI/Nature ML papers is toward explicit, structured limitation sections.

- **Closing sentences tend toward outlook + sharing/deployment, not result restatement**. MolE: "an initial step towards a foundation model"; Pai: "we share our foundation model and reproducible workflows"; FunSearch: "we envision that automatically-tailored algorithms will soon become common practice"; ChemCrow: "valuable assistants in chemical laboratories". Strong-verb result-restatement closings are the minority.

- **Hedge density is low to medium, but always present**: "may extend", "approaching the performance", "initial step towards", "is likely to", "we envision", "tend to". The strongest claim verbs ("solves", "outperforms", "discovers", "demonstrates") are reserved for benchmarked results sentences; abstract-level verbs are softer ("show", "report", "introduce").

- **Cross-engineering analogies are a distinctive 2024-25 NMI hook**. SemanticLens opens with the aeroplane/component analogy. FunSearch frames itself with the "easy to evaluate, hard to solve" Kolmogorov-complexity allusion. The trend: ML methods papers reach *outside* ML for their hook (engineering, theoretical CS, clinical workflows) rather than citing prior ML.

- **Quantitative claims combine raw counts with human-anchored references**. AlphaGeometry: "25/30 vs 10/30 vs gold medallist 25.9". FunSearch: "0.03 % off lower bound" + "first discoveries on established open problems". MolE: "10 of 22 ADMET tasks". The format is *count-with-comparator-and-anchor*, not standalone deltas.

- **Shared paper-architecture template** across all six: (1) field framing / analogy hook; (2) data or evaluation bottleneck; (3) named-method introduction in S3-S4 of abstract; (4) one mechanism sentence; (5) one or two benchmarked-result sentences; (6) generalisation / deployment claim. Variation lies almost entirely in where the *interpretability* or *limitation* sentence lands and whether a meta-property (interpretability of programs, robustness to perturbation) is given its own abstract sentence.

- **Reproducibility/sharing artefacts are foregrounded in the abstract or closing**: SemanticLens abstract names code+demo URLs; Pai et al. closes on reproducible workflows; ChemCrow ships an open-source platform; AlphaGeometry releases code/model. This is now near-universal in Nature-family ML papers and reads as a *credibility signal* rather than an afterthought.
